Why birds fly into glass

Reflections

Glass reflects more as you meet it at a slant. A pane that mirrors a tree or the sky shows a bird a place to fly to.

See-through spaces

Glass lobbies and glass corners let a bird see daylight and trees on the far side, so it looks like a gap to fly through.

Where birds fly

Most strikes happen low, near trees, where birds move between crowns. A head-on approach is worse than a glancing one.

From a sketch of the site to a work order

  1. Describe the site

    Buildings, how much of each facade is glass and what kind, see-through floors, and the trees around them.

  2. Simulate the flights

    Birds fly between tree crowns and across the site. At every pane they meet, the model traces the reflection and the view through the glass.

  3. Choose the markers

    For any budget, an exact optimiser picks interior or exterior markers pane by pane, allowing for setup visits and lifts.
